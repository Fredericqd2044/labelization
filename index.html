About the Project

Participants are invited to create labels for anything they notice in the park: a stone, a bench, a tree, a sound, a patch of grass, a piece of trash, or an everyday encounter. These labels may take the form of observations, memories, questions, personal interpretations, or cultural knowledge. Together, they allow the park to become a living guide shaped by visitors, residents, objects, and non-human beings.

The labels are documented and developed into an online archive and interactive guide, where visitors can browse the park through a map-based interface and encounter multiple interpretations of the same place or object.

LABEL-ization asks: What can be included in a guide, an exhibition, or an archive? Who gets to decide what matters? And how can labeling become a shared practice of attention, care, and interpretation?

Who Gets to Define?

In museums, labels function as authoritative tools that define what an object is, how it should be understood, and why it matters. However, they often condense complex histories into singular narratives, reinforcing institutional control over interpretation and meaning.

LABEL-ization aims to rethink the authority of the museum and its systems of selection and narrative construction through the open and collective act of labeling. What can be included in a museum? Who decides, and what do they represent? This project reframes labeling as a form of co-creation.
